Image sources for the introduction to control theory
===================================================

- `residual_network.tikz`: original vector diagram of a residual layer,
  adding the identity branch to the learned increment `h * g_k(x_k)`.
- `ds_gradient_descent.pdf`, `ds_wave.pdf`, `ds_transport.pdf`: original
  mathematical plots generated by `../make_figures.py` in the course
  palette. Gradient descent uses `F(w) = w^2 / 2`, step size `eta = 0.35`, and
  `w_0 = 2.4`. The wave plot uses the exact d'Alembert solution on the real line,
  `y(t,x) = (g(x-t) + g(x+t)) / 2`, with
  `g(x) = exp(-((x-0.5)/0.075)^2)` and zero initial velocity. Transport uses
  `y(t,x) = exp(-((x-t-0.18)/0.065)^2)` at speed `c = 1`.
- `residual_network_control.tikz`: the residual layer of `residual_network.tikz`
  with the increment `h * sigma(W_k x_k + b_k)`, its parameters `(W_k, b_k)`
  entering as the control in the slide colour `ctrl`.
- `ctrl_pendulum.pdf`, `ctrl_heat.pdf`: original figures for the controlled
  examples, generated by `../make_figures.py`, with the control in
  `P.ORANGE` (the colour `ctrl` of the slides). The pendulum is a schematic with
  the motor torque `u(t)` at the pivot. The heat plot is the exact sine-series
  solution of `y_t = y_xx + 1_omega u` on `(0,1)` with zero boundary values,
  `y(0,.) = 0`, `omega = (0.2, 0.4)` and constant `u = 25`.
- `brownian_motion.gif`, `brownian_motion.pdf`, `brownian_motion_poster.png`:
  original simulation of a planar Brownian path, generated by
  `../make_figures.py` with NumPy seed 42. The 1,200 independent Gaussian
  increments have covariance `dt * I`, with `dt = 6 / 1200`; plotted segments
  interpolate the sampled positions. The blue curve is the path so far, the
  orange dot the current position, and the black dot the origin. The GIF and
  140-page vector PDF share a 20 fps timeline, including a one-second final hold.
  The slide embeds the PDF frames using `animate`; its completed-path poster
  remains visible in viewers without animation support.
- `traffic_light.svg`: traffic-light icon by Marcin Floryan, listed as public
  domain geometry on [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Traffic_lights_icon.svg).
  The unmodified SVG is retained; `traffic_light.png` is rendered for LaTeX with
  `convert -density 480 -background none traffic_light.svg traffic_light.png`.
- `ising_spins.tikz`: original vector diagram of four classical Ising spins,
  showing one accepted spin flip. The finite-state model and update description
  follow [Carnegie Mellon lecture notes on the Ising model and Metropolis updates](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~gautam/c/2024-387/notes/06-metropolis-hastings.html).
- `markov_chain.tikz`: original vector diagram of a two-state weather model.
  The transition probabilities are illustrative, with rows
  `(0.8, 0.2)` and `(0.4, 0.6)` for states `(sun, rain)`.
- `water_clock_ctesibius.jpg`: historical illustration of Ctesibius' water
  clock, as used in the [Water clock article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_clock).
  From Abraham Rees' *Cyclopædia* (1819), probably illustrated by John Farey Jr.
  [Source and public-domain notice](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Clepsydra-Diagram-Fancy.jpeg).
  Downloaded without modification. Both historical device pictures have their
  sources recorded here rather than displayed on the slide.
- `governor_watt_diagram.jpg`: Watt governor and steam-valve diagram,
  attributed on Wikimedia Commons to James Watt, reworked by S. Wetzel.
  [Source and public-domain notice](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WattReg.jpg).
  Downloaded without modification; displayed in place of `governor_maxwell.png`.
- `maxwell_1868_equations.png`: screenshot of the upper portion of printed
  page 276 in J. C. Maxwell, *On Governors*, Proceedings of the Royal Society
  of London 16 (1868), 270–283. The excerpt shows equations (10)–(12), the
  oscillatory modes, and the stability condition.
  [Original paper, scanned PDF hosted by the University of Edinburgh](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/maxwell1.pdf).
  The original work is in the public domain. Rendered from PDF page 7 with:

  ```sh
  pdftoppm -f 7 -l 7 -r 240 -x 290 -y 130 -W 1450 -H 1165 \
    -singlefile -png maxwell1.pdf maxwell_1868_equations
  ```

- `stock_market.svg`: daily Dow Jones closing values around the 1987 crash,
  chart by Autopilot, released into the public domain.
  [Source, data provenance and license](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Black_Monday_Dow_Jones.svg).
  The unmodified SVG is retained as the source. `stock_market.png` is its
  rasterized version for inclusion in LaTeX, generated with:

  ```sh
  convert -density 240 -background white stock_market.svg stock_market.png
  ```

The stock-market assets are retained from an earlier version of the lecture;
the current finite-state examples are traffic lights, Ising spins and a Markov
chain.
